# Rooted Commons v3.0 — product and dashboard rationale

This document should travel with the v3.0 codebase so future changes preserve the reasoning behind the member experience, not just the current layout.

## What the dashboard is for

The member dashboard is deliberately not designed as a conventional ecommerce account page.

Rooted Commons is trying to help members experience themselves as participants in a local food network rather than passive customers. The dashboard therefore tells a progression:

**I belong → I commit → I connect locally → I get involved → together, we create wider change.**

The four dashboard cards each represent one part of that progression.

### 1. Your Membership — I belong

This is the member's identity and immediate account state.

It contains:
- the member greeting;
- Founder / Member badge;
- Member Credit;
- Browse this week's market;
- membership duration (`x weeks supporting local producers`);
- perks already unlocked and future perks with countdowns.

Membership duration sits immediately before the perks because time spent supporting the network is what explains the unlocking progression.

The card should feel like membership in a shared project, not a loyalty-points account.

### 2. Your Weekly Commitment — I commit

The regular contribution is the dependable economic commitment the network can plan around.

This card explains:
- the member's weekly-equivalent commitment;
- how to set up or maintain the regular payment;
- their unique RC-x payment reference;
- payment/account history where useful.

The commitment is intentionally distinct from ordinary shopping spend. Predictable member demand is part of the infrastructure Rooted Commons is trying to build.

### 3. Your Collection Point — I connect locally

The collection point is the member's physical connection to the network.

The card should remain practical and uncluttered:
- location image;
- collection time;
- address;
- ability to change collection preferences;
- link to the collection-point map.

This is not just fulfilment logistics: over time collection points may become places where members encounter producers, partner organisations and each other.

### 4. Your Involvement — I take part

This card records ways the member participates in the local food network.

The initial 2 × 2 counters are:
- money redirected locally;
- days volunteering in the local food network;
- workshops attended;
- events attended.

These should remain measures of participation, not points, rankings or competitive gamification.

`Redirected locally` is intentional wording: it emphasises that choosing where money flows is an active decision rather than merely recording consumer expenditure.

The card ends with:

**You're part of something bigger.**

and a link into the wider network story.

## Collective impact — together, we create wider change

Do not try to allocate every outcome of partner organisations to an individual member.

Rooted Commons sits inside a network in which farms grow and donate food, volunteers contribute time, community organisations create social value, and partner businesses may give profits to wider projects. Those outcomes belong to the network and should be told as a collective story with appropriate attribution.

The dashboard should therefore bridge into a broader network-impact page rather than creating a competing `Your impact / Our impact` split.

That wider story can eventually include:
- food grown;
- food donated or redistributed;
- volunteer hours;
- workshops and community events;
- money circulated through local producers;
- grants, profits or resources donated by network partners;
- qualitative stories showing what partners and members make possible together.

The distinction is:

**Your Involvement** records how a member participates.

**Our network's collective impact** tells what becomes possible through everyone acting together.

## Design implications

- Prefer belonging and participation language over conventional ecommerce language.
- Do not turn participation counters into leaderboards, points or rewards.
- Keep individual action connected visually and narratively to the wider network.
- Preserve the distinction between Member Credit, weekly commitment and ordinary order spend.
- Make practical account actions easy, but do not let transactional information consume the whole dashboard.
- When adding a new metric, ask whether it tells a meaningful story about participation or merely fills space.
