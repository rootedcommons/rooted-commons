# Member Credit — internal regulatory perimeter note

**Status:** Internal working assessment  
**Date:** 5 August 2026  
**Owner:** Roots to Fruits CIC / Rooted Commons  
**Purpose:** Record why the current Member Credit model does not presently appear to require FCA e-money/payment-services authorisation, and identify changes that should trigger a fresh review.

> This is an internal compliance assessment, not a legal opinion. Seek specialist UK payments advice if the model expands materially or there is doubt about the regulatory perimeter.

## Current model

As currently designed:

- members make regular payments to Roots to Fruits CIC;
- the amount received is recorded in Baserow as `Member Credit`;
- credit is account-based and electronically recorded;
- it can be used only to purchase goods through the Rooted Commons weekly market;
- members cannot transfer credit to one another;
- credit cannot be used as a general-purpose payment method;
- it does not earn interest and is not presented as a savings product;
- Rooted Commons/Roots to Fruits CIC is the seller to the member: members do not use the credit to make a separate payment directly to each producer or collection-point partner;
- producers/suppliers are paid by Roots to Fruits CIC under the supply arrangements, rather than receiving a member's RC credit as their own means of payment;
- collection points do not accept RC credit as an independent payment instrument;
- any partner voucher offered when a member leaves is an agreed way of resolving remaining credit, not a standing facility allowing Member Credit to be spent directly across partner businesses.

## Why the distinction matters

Under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, e-money is broadly electronically stored monetary value representing a claim on the issuer, issued on receipt of funds for making payment transactions, and **accepted as a means of payment by someone other than the issuer**.

The FCA's PERG 3A guidance confirms that account-based prepaid schemes can be e-money where the stored value is used to purchase goods or services directly from third-party merchants.

On the present Rooted Commons model, the strongest reason the balance may fall outside the e-money definition is therefore **not simply the Limited Network Exclusion**. It is that the Member Credit appears to operate more like closed-loop retailer/store credit: the member prepays Roots to Fruits CIC and later uses that credit against goods sold to them by Roots to Fruits CIC. The underlying suppliers do not themselves accept the Member Credit from the member.

The FCA gives a comparable example for basic retailer gift cards: where the issuer is the retailer and the card can only obtain goods/services from that retailer, the FCA says this is not a payment instrument for PSR purposes because the customer has paid the retailer in advance for the goods/services.

This distinction should be preserved deliberately.

## Limited Network Exclusion as a secondary consideration

If the model changed so that Member Credit became a payment instrument accepted by third-party providers, the Limited Network Exclusion (LNE) could become relevant.

The LNE can cover instruments usable only:
1. on the issuer's premises;
2. within a limited network of service providers that have direct commercial agreements with the issuer; or
3. for a very limited range of goods or services.

FCA guidance expressly gives membership cards usable only for goods/services offered by a specific club or organisation as a possible limited-network example.

However, the FCA also warns that:
- being restricted by terms and conditions alone is not enough; restrictions should be functional where practicable;
- instruments usable across an unlimited range of goods/services are not limited;
- a network of listed merchants that is continuously growing may cease to be sufficiently limited;
- whether a network is limited depends on its actual structure and scale.

Rooted Commons should therefore **not rely casually on the LNE as the primary basis for the current model**. The present closed-loop seller relationship is cleaner.

## FCA notification threshold

If Rooted Commons ever does rely on the LNE and the total value of transactions made through the relevant excluded instrument exceeds **€1 million in any 12-month period**, FCA notification requirements apply. The FCA says notification must be made within 28 days after the threshold conditions are met and repeated as required while the conditions continue.

This is not remotely close to current operating scale, but should remain on the review checklist.

## Design rules to preserve the current position

Unless a fresh regulatory review is undertaken:

1. **Keep Roots to Fruits CIC as seller/merchant of record** for Rooted Commons member purchases.
2. **Do not allow members to transfer Member Credit** to other members.
3. **Do not allow cash withdrawal on demand** as a wallet feature. This does not affect refunds or repayment required by consumer law or agreed when membership ends.
4. **Do not allow partner businesses to accept RC credit directly at their own tills/websites.**
5. Suppliers and collection points should continue to be paid separately by Roots to Fruits CIC rather than receiving Member Credit from members.
6. Do not issue a general-purpose payment card or virtual card backed by Member Credit.
7. Do not market Member Credit as money, banking, savings, a payment account, or a general wallet.
8. Keep Member Credit usable only within the Rooted Commons purchasing relationship.
9. A partner voucher used by agreement to settle a leaving member's remaining balance should remain an exit arrangement, not become routine interoperability between RC credit and partner payment systems.
10. Keep an eye on aggregate balances as a practical consumer-protection and cash-flow matter even though balance size alone does not determine the regulatory classification.

## Mandatory review triggers

Obtain specialist payments/regulatory advice before implementing any of the following:

- members can spend their RC balance directly with Arden & Scott, Karuna Coffee, Farnham Community Farm or another partner;
- partners can redeem RC credit with Roots to Fruits CIC;
- Member Credit becomes transferable between members;
- a physical/virtual payment card is introduced;
- Member Credit can be spent outside the Rooted Commons checkout;
- the network is deliberately expanded into a marketplace of independently accepting merchants;
- cash withdrawals/redemptions become a normal wallet feature rather than refunds/exit settlements;
- another organisation begins issuing the stored value;
- the legal seller/merchant-of-record structure changes;
- transaction volume approaches the FCA LNE notification threshold;
- the product is marketed increasingly as a wallet/payment service rather than prepaid purchasing credit.

## Current conclusion

On the facts above, there is a credible basis for treating Member Credit as **closed-loop prepaid retailer credit rather than e-money** because it is used against purchases from the same legal entity that receives and records the prepayment.

If third parties begin accepting the balance as payment in their own right, that analysis changes. At that point the e-money/payment-services perimeter and the Limited Network Exclusion should be reviewed before launch.

The operational design should therefore preserve the present distinction: **a network of suppliers is not the same thing as a network of merchants accepting Member Credit.**
